    About this map, sources and limits
    • Delivery days: USPS service standards from PostalPro. These are USPS's own targets, from the day mail is accepted. Real delivery can be slower.
    • Accuracy: days are looked up by the first three digits of each ZIP code, so every ZIP that shares a prefix gets the same color. Since 2025, USPS adds one day for some ZIPs more than 50 miles from their regional processing plant, so a few ZIPs may be one day slower than shown. Check a specific pair at usps.com/service-standards.
    • Mailing day: USPS counts delivery days starting the day after your mail is accepted and skips Sundays and federal holidays. Picking a day under “Mailed on” shows the day of the week it should arrive: a 3-day letter mailed Friday arrives Tuesday. Mail dropped in a box after the last pickup, or on Sunday, is accepted the next business day. Holidays add a day and are not shown.
    • How often it's on time: USPS's own quarterly service performance reports, by district (Periodicals: by area). They show the share of mail delivered within the target, and within 1 and 3 extra days. Letters use the Single-Piece First-Class Mail results; Marketing Mail uses end-to-end results. USPS publishes no regional results for Priority Mail or Ground Advantage. Districts come from USPS's ZIP-to-district list.
    • City labels: Natural Earth populated places (public domain). Major cities show on the full map; more appear as you zoom in.
    • ZIP shapes: U.S. Census Bureau 2020 ZIP Code Tabulation Areas. They approximate ZIP codes; PO-box-only ZIPs have no shape.
    • Map: Equal Earth projection, which keeps areas true. Alaska, Hawaii and the territories are shown in separate boxes, each at its own scale.
