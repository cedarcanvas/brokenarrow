Avalanche deaths in the United States

Since the winter of 1950–51, avalanches have killed 1,255 people in the United States. Deaths rose from about 4 a winter in the 1950s to 29 a winter in the 2000s as more people skied, climbed and rode snowmobiles into the backcountry. The last ten winters averaged 24. Colorado has lost 328, more than any other state.

About the data

  • Records: Colorado Avalanche Information Center, US avalanche accident data (October 2025 edition). CAIC asks that you cite it when you use the data.
  • Each record is one avalanche in which at least one person died. A winter runs from October to September; 2024–25 is the winter that ended in 2025.
  • CAIC sorts victims into 24 activities. They are grouped into seven here:
  • Accident reports: report links go to the investigation published by the Colorado Avalanche Information Center, which also hosts reports from other states' avalanche centers, or by the Utah Avalanche Center. They are matched to these records by state, date, place and number killed, and a link is left off where the match is uncertain.
  • Dots use the coordinates in CAIC's records. Accidents without coordinates count toward their state's shading only. Older place names were stored in capital letters and are shown in normal case here.
  • Shaded relief: Ridgeline Maps, from USGS 3DEP elevation via the AWS Terrain Tiles open dataset. Mountain range and peak names: Natural Earth. State outlines: U.S. Census Bureau, via us-atlas.
